Ranked builds: theoretical damage model

Every champion's best build under the selected scenario, side by side: the top row of its own ranking, so each champion brings the boots and five items that kill this target fastest (or, overall, that do best on all three at once). Same level, same stat dummies and same fight lengths for everyone. Ranked by expected kill time, fastest first; a champion that never kills the target comes after every one that does, most damage first. Overall, champions that kill all three targets come before those that leave one standing, then the geometric mean of the three kill times decides, so each target counts equally in percentage terms. Equal times, to the hundredth of a second, share a rank. Select a column heading to sort by it, and again to reverse: a target's column orders the champions by how the build shown does in that one fight (that target's own scenario ranks each champion's best build against it instead), and # stays the scenario's rank whatever the order. This compares damage on a stat dummy and nothing else: range, safety, crowd control, mobility, sustain and how hard a combo is to land count for nothing, so an assassin's burst and a marksman's sustained damage are compared as if both always connect. Most kits here were written by a model and reviewed by nobody (tagged unreviewed): their rotations can be wrong, and a kill at 0.00s means a draft lands its whole opening combo in the fight's first instant. Select a row for where that build's damage comes from and what its kit assumes; select the champion's name for its full build ranking.
First-principles simulation: exact stat sheets, ability formulas, item procs, expected-value crit, no match data. Pick a scenario; every item-pool combination is simulated against that stat dummy and ranked by expected kill time. That differs from raw kill time only for builds carrying an execute: crit here is an expected value, so the health curve is fixed and an execute window always catches the target; real crit would land in it only some of the time, so the two timelines are blended by that probability. Ties on the same attack are broken by how much damage the build had to spare. The three targets are simulated together, so each row also shows the same build's kill time against the other two; the overall scenario ranks the builds on all three at once, by the geometric mean of those kill times: each target counts equally in percentage terms, and a build has to kill every target to rank above one that leaves any standing. Click a build to see where its damage comes from against each target. The top 10 builds of each scenario number their items in a suggested buy order, boots bought early: the order whose partial builds kill fastest along the way, each fought at an earlier level against an earlier-game target and weighted by the gold to the next item. Rod of Ages always goes first (its stacks need time owned); Seraph's and Muramana never do (their Tear needs time to stack). Damage only: lane safety and defense don't count.
First-principles survival model for tanks, no match data. The tank takes the damage and never fights back. Each attacker is one of the modeled carries with its current best overall build, fought exactly as in the damage ranking; the target is the tank's build, with its health, armor, magic resist, and everything its items and kit do while it takes damage. That covers damage reductions, shields, Death's Dance's delay, Guardian Angel and Zhonya's stasis, resists that grow in combat, regeneration and heals. Every build of the tank pool is fought against each attacker and ranked by time to die, longest first; a build still standing when the fight ends ranks above every build that fell, by the time the damage it took would have needed. The overall scenario ranks on every attacker at once: builds that fewer attackers kill come first, then the geometric mean of the times. Maximum Dosage goes out at the health threshold (every 10%) that keeps the tank alive longest against that attacker, and always when a hit would otherwise kill him. Lifesteal, omnivamp and anything else that needs the tank's own hits count for nothing. Click a build to see what hit him and what kept him up.

    How this comparison works

    Each champion uses its best full three-item build from every legal combination in the selected fight. The leaderboard starts with 2★ 4-costs and first trait breakpoints. Best available uses 3★ for 1–3 costs and 2★ for 4–5 costs. The 3★ board only includes 1–3 costs. Formation and trait settings stay the same across the board; active trait bonuses still depend on each champion.

    Damage ranks by time to clear all targets, then damage for champions that cannot clear. Carries are protected; fighters and assassins also take attacks and casts from their targets. Use the role filter to compare the same pressure conditions. DPS is averaged over the simulated fight. All damage targets start with team-supplied Sunder and Shred.

    2nd BIS gap compares each champion’s best and second-best distinct three-item builds. It shows how much slower the second build clears, or how much less damage it deals when neither build clears. Tank gaps compare time held, using twice the incoming damage when both builds reach the regular limit. Hover over a gap for the second build’s items and exact results. A dash means a percentage cannot be measured; reaching both survival limits is an observed tie.

    Tanks rank by how long they hold the enemy formation, including on-death bodies. 60s+ means the fight limit was reached. Those champions are compared again with twice the incoming damage; reaching both limits remains a tie. Effective healing, shields and stuns contribute to survival. Team utility does not change these ranks.

    These are theoretical fights, not match win rates. Equal performance shares a rank, and filters keep the full board’s ranks. Ranks use unrounded results; displayed times and damage are rounded. Select a champion for its exact fight, alternatives and ability assumptions.

    Typical lobby estimates need published play-rate data for this patch.

    Play rates and star levels estimate opponent copies across the whole shop pool. The default uses 7 other players and 50% of final-board holdings as a 4-1 assumption. Change this percentage to match your lobby; the source does not capture 4-1 benches or temporary holds.

    How trait scoring works

    Every reached trait counts, including solo traits that activate with one unit. The first reached breakpoint activates a trait; each higher reached breakpoint adds one extra tier. Unfinished breakpoints add no score.

    More active traits scores 3 × active traits + extra tiers. Higher verticals scores active traits + 3 × extra tiers. These are unit-selection heuristics; scores do not estimate combat strength.

    Every board fills exactly 8 team slots with distinct units, keeps all selected cores and kept units, activates at least one trait for each core, and meets your frontline minimum. Solo traits count toward core coverage. A unit that occupies two team slots is counted accordingly.

    With no champions selected, browse all pairs of the chosen cost. With one selected, browse every same-cost partner for that champion. With two or more selected, keep them all as the core. Any combination of roles is allowed. A 2-cost comp can use at most two 4-cost units; a 3-cost comp can use at most three. The cap includes kept units and support swaps. 4-cost cores have no additional 4-cost limit.

    The search shows up to 6 varied support packages per core group. Comps found with several core groups appear once. These are promising boards from a bounded search, rather than every possible 8-slot roster or a proven global optimum. Sorting compares the comps found by this search. Expand a comp’s swaps to see which reached breakpoints and scores change.

    How expected gold is calculated

    Start at level 8 with zero copies of this exact roster. For 2- and 3-cost comps, every unit of the chosen cost needs 9 copies (3★), including supports; every other unit needs 1 copy (1★). For 4-cost comps, every 4-cost needs 3 copies (2★), and every other unit needs 1 copy. Kept-unit locks choose the roster but do not subtract owned copies from this estimate.

    The total is the average gold spent on shop refreshes plus buying the required units. It collects the whole roster together, accounting for units found in the same shop and copies you buy leaving the bag. It is not the sum of separate unit estimates.

    Uncontested gold assumes you are the only player taking units from the bag. Typical lobby gold subtracts the opponent holdings in the scouting controls from every champion’s bag, then collects your roster from the remaining pool. Other players’ holdings stay fixed during the estimate. Missing copies can make a roster impossible under those assumptions.

    Typical lobby holdings use cached tactics.tools play rates and star-level copy equivalents, scaled by the other-player count and final-board percentage, then rounded to whole copies. Special upgrades and generated units can differ from standard copy equivalents; manual scouting replaces the estimate for a unit. Typical lobby gold is an approximation of your lobby. Both estimates show sampling precision when simulation is used.

    Every refresh costs 2 gold. No free shops, leveling costs, income, Wisp purchases, or augments are included. Shop-changing traits are assumed inactive during the rolldown.

    Set 18 alternates shops with 4 and 5 visible champions because a Wisp hides the rightmost champion every other shop. The estimate averages both possible starting phases. Gold is an expectation for the named roster; accepting substitutes can reduce the cost.

    Item pool

    Every ranked build is three of these, repeats allowed. Each item's plain stats always count; the tag says which passive the engine models, and a dimmed chip is stats only.       How builds are ranked

      Every three-item combination is simulated against the selected enemy formation. Its target count, roles, order, positions and defenses are shown in the test fight. Targets marked as main tanks use fixed benchmark HP and resistances; supporting target defenses use the saved group medians. Fighters take attacks and casts drawn from those median stats. Tank pressure combines median frontline damage with damage calibrated from 2★, three-item carry builds; the selected preset sets the backline damage mix and timing. Damage accounts for armor, magic resist, Sunder, Shred, expected-value crit, Precision and damage amplification.

      Carries are protected and rank by time to defeat all targets, then damage. Fighters and assassins take pressure from the frontline; the backline is a damage target only. They rank by kill time, then damage before dying. Tanks face attackers that remain alive throughout the survival test, with enemy heal cut, Sunder and Shred active. Stuns interrupt only the enemies they reach, and scheduled casts resume afterward. Tanks rank by how long they hold enemies, including on-death bodies. Builds reaching the displayed fight limit are compared again with twice the incoming damage; reaching both limits remains a tie. Shields, effective healing, durability and stuns contribute to survival.

      Tank effective HP (EHP) shows the opening capacity to take physical or magic ability damage after enemy Sunder and Shred. It excludes healing, shields and protection that applies only to attacks. Team healing, shields and damage are shown separately; survival rankings do not measure how quickly allied carries win the fight.

      Clumped and Spread use different saved positions. Each ability's radius and target-selection rules determine its coverage; a wide enough radius can include backliners. Nearby-only effects follow the frontline grouping, while nearest-target and whole-board abilities retain their stated rules. Exact movement paths are not simulated. Trait settings apply the champion's modeled traits at no breakpoint, the first breakpoint, or the highest non-prismatic breakpoint. Unit numbers come from the set's data with patch-note corrections; individual ability assumptions appear in the fight details.
