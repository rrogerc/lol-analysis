Ranked builds — theoretical damage model

First-principles simulation: exact stat sheets, ability formulas, item procs, expected-value crit — no match data. Pick a scenario; every item-pool combination is simulated against that stat dummy and ranked by expected kill time. That differs from raw kill time only for builds carrying an execute: crit here is an expected value, so the health curve is fixed and an execute window always catches the target — real crit would land in it only some of the time, so the two timelines are blended by that probability. Ties on the same attack are broken by how much damage the build had to spare. The three targets are simulated together, so each row also shows the same build's kill time against the other two; the overall scenario ranks the builds on all three at once, by the geometric mean of those kill times — each target counts equally in percentage terms, and a build has to kill every target to rank above one that leaves any standing. Click a build to see where its damage comes from against each target.

    How this comparison works

    Each champion uses its best full three-item build from every legal combination in the selected fight. Best available uses 3★ for 1–3 costs and 2★ for 4–5 costs. The 3★ board only includes 1–3 costs. Formation and trait settings stay the same across the board; active trait bonuses still depend on each champion.

    Damage ranks by time to clear all three targets, then damage for champions that cannot clear. Carries are protected; fighters and assassins also take attacks and casts from their targets. Use the role filter to compare the same pressure conditions. DPS is averaged over the simulated fight. All damage targets start with team-supplied Sunder and Shred.

    Tanks rank by how long they hold five attackers, including on-death bodies. 60s+ means the fight limit was reached. Those champions are compared again with twice the incoming damage; reaching both limits remains a tie. Effective healing, shields and stuns contribute to survival. Team utility does not change these ranks.

    These are theoretical fights, not match win rates. Equal performance shares a rank, and filters keep the full board’s ranks. Ranks use unrounded results; displayed times and damage are rounded. Select a champion for its exact fight, alternatives and ability assumptions.

    How these boards are scored

    Boards have a main carry and tank of the chosen cost, and one shared budget for completed items.

    The allies share one fight clock, with the board’s actual trait counts and items. Enemies target individual champions, then change targets after a death. Champions attack and cast only while alive and able to act; burns and on-death bodies can persist. Healing, shields and crowd control affect the same fight.

    Both teams use actual champions, traits and items in the same combat engine. Every candidate faces the same fixed reference boards twice, once with each side acting first at simultaneous events. These simplified formations and targeting rules do not reproduce every live-game interaction. Results are benchmark comparisons, not live win rates.

    The selected clumped or spread formation applies to both teams. This is a coarse positioning assumption; full hex movement is not simulated.

    Reference boards use the same completed-item budget as your board. Their champions, stars and item priorities are fixed before the search.

    Held-out checks use separate opponent boards after the items have been chosen. Those results do not influence item selection or composition ranks.

    The current healing assumptions allow burn, trait and item damage to trigger healing, and persistent effects can heal allies after their source dies. Expanded held-out results show sensitivity to stricter, unverified healing rules.

    Multiple Hextech Gunblades on one holder combine ally healing before selecting a recipient. Per-copy recipient timing is unverified and is not covered by the held-out healing check.

    Encounter DPS averages actual damage over each whole fight, including time lost to deaths. Frontline held is the time until the last allied frontliner dies or the fight ends, averaged across opponents. Expand a board to see individual fights and each champion’s contribution.

    Boards rank only by their win rate in the ranking tests. Equal win rates share a rank; remaining health, damage and clear time explain the fights without breaking ties. The search compares promising boards and item allocations; it does not prove a global optimum.

    Purchase gold counts the copies on the finished board. It does not include leveling costs or the gold and odds required to roll the board.

    These are individual champion item tests. Composition rankings use shared team fights with the board’s actual traits, targeting and deaths, so the best items can differ.

      How builds are ranked

      Every three-item combination is simulated. Carries and fighters face three stat dummies: a heavy tank first, a typical tank second, and a non-tank behind them. Tanks face three frontline tanks and two protected backline carries. The first enemy tank has fixed benchmark defenses, shown in the test fight; the other defenses use this set's median 2★ stats. Fighters face attacks and casts drawn from those median stats. Tank pressure combines the median frontline damage with damage calibrated from 2★, three-item carry builds. The selected preset sets the backline damage mix and timing. Damage accounts for armor, magic resist, sunder, shred, expected-value crit, Precision and damage amplification.

      Carries are ranked by time to kill all three dummies, then damage. Fighters and assassins face dummies that hit back, and are ranked by kill time, then damage before dying. Tanks face five attackers that cannot die for up to a minute, with enemy heal cut, sunder and shred active throughout. The backline keeps attacking through frontline stuns; abilities with explicit long-range or global targeting can still reach it. Enemy casts are delayed by stuns and resume afterward. Tanks rank by how long they hold the enemies, including on-death bodies. Builds that reach 60s are shown as 60s+ and compared in another fight with twice the incoming damage; builds that reach both limits remain tied. Shields, effective healing, durability and stuns contribute to survival.

      Tank effective HP (EHP) shows the opening capacity to take physical or magic ability damage after enemy sunder and shred. It excludes healing, shields and protection that applies only to attacks. Team healing, shields and damage are shown separately; survival rankings do not measure how quickly allied carries win the fight.

      Spread keeps enemies apart, so most local area effects reach only the current target; clumped enemies can take splash damage together, subject to each ability's target limit. In tank tests, this setting changes the frontline formation; the backline stays outside nearby effects in both formations. Abilities that select several nearest enemies can still reach multiple frontliners when spread. Trait settings apply the champion's modeled traits at no breakpoint, the first breakpoint, or the highest non-prismatic breakpoint. Unit numbers come from the set's data with patch-note corrections; individual ability assumptions appear in the fight details.
