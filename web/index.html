Ranked builds — theoretical damage model

First-principles simulation: exact stat sheets, ability formulas, item procs, expected-value crit — no match data. Pick a scenario; every item-pool combination is simulated against that stat dummy and ranked by expected kill time. That differs from raw kill time only for builds carrying an execute: crit here is an expected value, so the health curve is fixed and an execute window always catches the target — real crit would land in it only some of the time, so the two timelines are blended by that probability. Ties on the same attack are broken by how much damage the build had to spare. The three targets are simulated together, so each row also shows the same build's kill time against the other two; the overall scenario ranks the builds on all three at once, by the geometric mean of those kill times — each target counts equally in percentage terms, and a build has to kill every target to rank above one that leaves any standing. Click a build to see where its damage comes from against each target.

      How builds are ranked

      Every three-item combination is simulated. Carries and fighters face three stat dummies: a heavy tank first, a typical tank second, and a non-tank behind them. Tanks face three frontline tanks and two protected backline carries. The first enemy tank has fixed benchmark defenses, shown in the test fight; the other defenses use this set's median 2★ stats. Fighters face attacks and casts drawn from those median stats. Tank pressure combines the median frontline damage with damage calibrated from 2★, three-item carry builds. The selected preset sets the backline damage mix and timing. Damage accounts for armor, magic resist, sunder, shred, expected-value crit, Precision and damage amplification.

      Carries are ranked by time to kill all three dummies, then damage. Fighters and assassins face dummies that hit back, and are ranked by kill time, then damage before dying. Tanks face five attackers that cannot die for up to a minute, with enemy heal cut, sunder and shred active throughout. The backline keeps attacking through frontline stuns; abilities with explicit long-range or global targeting can still reach it. Enemy casts are delayed by stuns and resume afterward. Tanks rank by how long they hold the enemies, including on-death bodies. Builds that reach 60s are shown as 60s+ and compared in another fight with twice the incoming damage; builds that reach both limits remain tied. Shields, effective healing, durability and stuns contribute to survival.

      Tank effective HP (EHP) shows the opening capacity to take physical or magic ability damage after enemy sunder and shred. It excludes healing, shields and protection that applies only to attacks. Team healing, shields and damage are shown separately; survival rankings do not measure how quickly allied carries win the fight.

      Spread keeps enemies apart, so most local area effects reach only the current target; clumped enemies can take splash damage together, subject to each ability's target limit. In tank tests, this setting changes the frontline formation; the backline stays outside nearby effects in both formations. Abilities that select several nearest enemies can still reach multiple frontliners when spread. Trait settings apply the champion's modeled traits at no breakpoint, the first breakpoint, or the highest non-prismatic breakpoint. Unit numbers come from the set's data with patch-note corrections; individual ability assumptions appear in the fight details.
